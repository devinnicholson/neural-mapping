\title{Error Prediction Is Not Acquisition:\\A Controlled Study of Sparse-View Gaussian Neural Mapping}
\author{Devin Nicholson}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We test whether a model signal that predicts difficult views also improves
frame acquisition for sparse-view 3D Gaussian neural maps.  From 25 posed seed
observations, a frozen policy expands to 50 by combining the upper tail of a
rendered depth-gradient proxy with camera-pose novelty.  We compare it with
matched random, acquisition-time coverage, and pose-coverage rules on four
ICL-NUIM RGB-D trajectories.  The confirmatory protocol uses disjoint temporal
holdouts, two optimization seeds, raw metric poses, and held-out RGB, depth,
and fused surface outcomes.  The proxy detects high-error candidate views in
all eight seed models (mean AUROC 0.716) and improves mean PSNR by 0.637 dB and
LPIPS by 0.023 over random.  Nevertheless, the co-primary 5 cm surface F-score
changes by $-0.0016$, both trajectory-cluster intervals include zero, and pose
coverage is stronger under the seed-42 control comparison.  The preregistered
overall-support gate fails.  These results separate error ranking from useful
acquisition and show why image-space gains alone are insufficient evidence for
neural mapping policies.
\end{abstract}
