\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

The benchmark uses four synthetic trajectories through one scene model, not
four independent physical environments.  Two optimizer seeds characterize only
a narrow component of training variance.  Candidate poses are known in advance,
so the experiment does not measure tracking failure, motion cost, or online
planning latency.  The uncertainty signal is the spatial gradient of rendered
expected depth: a renderer-derived edge/structure heuristic, not a calibrated
posterior.  Its error-prediction diagnostics test empirical usefulness but do
not transform it into epistemic uncertainty.  Surface metrics are reconstructed
from held-out depth views rather than evaluated against the complete official
mesh, so unobserved
regions remain outside the metric.  Finally, the selector inherits the
representation and inductive biases of Splatfacto; conclusions need not transfer
to implicit radiance fields, point-based maps, or depth-supervised mapping.  The
confirmatory study compares against matched random, temporal-coverage, and
pose-coverage controls, but does not reimplement FisherRF, POp-GS, COVER, or
other contemporary active-mapping systems.  It therefore tests the frozen
heuristic's incremental value under a controlled budget, not state-of-the-art
superiority.

The study also does not isolate every causal component of the hybrid score.
The random and two coverage controls answer whether the complete frozen policy
adds value, but a new preregistered experiment would be needed to estimate
weight-specific effects without post-outcome tuning.  Exact billable GPU
durations were not emitted by the original runner, so the record reports GPU
class, job counts, iterations, and software versions but not reconstructed
GPU-hours.  Future runners should persist start, stop, and billable duration in
the same artifact as each metric.
