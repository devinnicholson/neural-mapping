\section{Method}

Let $S_0$ contain the initial posed frames, $C$ the eligible candidate pool, and
$B$ the target training budget.  A seed 3DGS model $f_{S_0}$ is trained from
$S_0$.  For candidate view $v$, the renderer produces a depth-gradient signal
$u_v(p)$ for pixel $p$.  Concretely, if $z_v$ is rendered expected depth, the
frozen proxy is the forward finite-difference magnitude
\begin{equation}
u_v(i,j)=\sqrt{|z_v(i,j)-z_v(i-1,j)|^2+|z_v(i,j)-z_v(i,j-1)|^2}.
\end{equation}
It is a deterministic priority heuristic, not a posterior variance or a
calibrated probability.  We define its upper-tail aggregate as
\begin{equation}
U(v)=\frac{1}{|Q_v|}\sum_{p\in Q_v}u_v(p),
\end{equation}
where $Q_v$ is the highest-scoring pixel decile.  This prevents a large smooth
background from overwhelming a spatially concentrated signal.  Computing
$U(v)$ requires only the candidate pose and the seed model's rendered depth;
candidate RGB and reference depth are reserved for post-hoc evaluation and
never enter the acquisition decision.

At each greedy step, let $D(v,S)$ be the Euclidean distance between the
candidate camera center and its nearest selected center.  Candidate scores and
distances are independently min--max normalized over the remaining pool to
$\widetilde U$ and $\widetilde D$ and combined as
\begin{equation}
A(v\mid S)=\lambda\widetilde U(v)+(1-\lambda)\widetilde D(v,S),
\end{equation}
with frozen $\lambda=0.35$.  We add the maximizing candidate to $S$ until
$|S|=B$.  The selected 50-frame set is retrained from scratch with the same
optimizer seed and iteration count as its random comparator.
We refer to this frozen hybrid as \method.

The controls expand the exact same seed set.  Random uses the nested frozen
split, trajectory coverage applies farthest-first selection in acquisition
index, and pose coverage applies farthest-first selection in camera-center
distance.  These controls distinguish a useful model signal from the benefits
of simple de-duplication and geometric coverage.
