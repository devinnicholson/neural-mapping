\section{Introduction}

Neural scene representations are commonly trained from whatever views happen
to be available.  In mapping, the acquisition budget is itself a decision:
which additional observation should be incorporated when rendering quality,
geometry, latency, and redundancy all matter?  This question is distinct from
building a better renderer.  A useful acquisition rule must transfer across
camera trajectories, outperform inexpensive coverage heuristics, and improve
map geometry rather than only image-space appearance.

We investigate an offline, pool-based version of this problem for 3D Gaussian
Splatting (3DGS).  A seed map renders every eligible candidate pose.  We
aggregate a prescribed renderer-derived signal over its highest-scoring pixel
decile and combine that score with distance from already selected camera
centers.  The resulting policy is simple enough to audit and exposes a direct
question: does model state contain acquisition information beyond trajectory or
pose coverage?

Our study is organized around falsifiable comparisons.  The policy and weight
are fixed using earlier development sequences.  Confirmatory evaluation uses
four trajectories, two training seeds, disjoint temporal-block
holdouts with guard bands, and matched 25-to-50-frame expansions.  We measure
PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, metric depth, and surface accuracy, completeness, Chamfer-L1,
and F-score.  Pixel samples are used to estimate per-frame metrics, but the
experimental unit is the trajectory-by-seed pair.

The central result is a mismatch.  The depth-gradient proxy consistently ranks
high-error candidates, and its selector improves average held-out appearance,
but neither metric surface geometry nor replication and control-superiority
gates support the acquisition hypothesis.  This is not resolved by choosing a
favorable image metric: the conclusion is fixed by prespecified co-primary
outcomes and every completed pair remains in the analysis.

The intended contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a reproducible acquisition protocol with fixed information boundaries
        and matched random, temporal-coverage, and pose-coverage controls;
  \item a renderer-derived depth-structure and pose-diversity selector evaluated
        unchanged across four RGB-D trajectories and two optimization seeds;
  \item a metric surface evaluator that fuses held-out predicted and reference
        depth in the published coordinate system; and
  \item a complete record of supportive, mixed, and negative outcomes, including
        uncertainty diagnostics and protocol corrections made before outcome
        inspection; and
  \item evidence that candidate-error discrimination does not by itself imply
        geometry-improving acquisition, motivating decision-aware objectives
        and stronger coverage controls.
\end{itemize}
