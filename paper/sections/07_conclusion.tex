\section{Conclusion}

We formulate sparse-view Gaussian neural mapping as a controlled acquisition
problem and evaluate one frozen uncertainty--pose policy against matched random
and non-learned coverage rules.  The study couples rendering metrics with raw
metric depth, fused surface geometry, uncertainty diagnostics, and explicit
decision gates.  The supported conclusion is narrow: rendered depth gradients
rank difficult candidate views and, when combined with pose novelty, improve
average held-out appearance over random in this benchmark.  The stronger claim
fails.  The policy does not improve average metric surface F-score, does not
meet the replication rule, and does not outperform direct coverage controls.
For active neural mapping, uncertainty or error detection should therefore be
validated through its downstream geometric decision effect, not accepted as a
surrogate endpoint.
